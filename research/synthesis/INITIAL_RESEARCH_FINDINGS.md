# Initial research findings: consolidated thematic reference

## Current research priority — 2026-10-16

**Working question:** How can adversarial web environments induce and sustain task-dependent navigation traps in autonomous AI web agents, and what determines whether agents escape, recover, or continue unproductive interaction?

The primary proposal concerns task-dependent retention and recovery, not a universal standalone-versus-agent vulnerability comparison. Classical traps motivate resource/navigation hazards; POTEMKIN already demonstrates navigation traps, TRAP demonstrates task persuasion and illustrates chained continuation, and tool-chain studies already exploit validation/progress pretexts. These findings constrain novelty. The [focused evidence map](THEMATIC_EVIDENCE_MAP.md#focused-trap-comparison) relates these existing records to supplemental Honeyval, LoginTrap, AGENTBAIT, Telephone Loop and WebStep evidence without adding canonical records.

**Analytical interpretation:** useful-looking prerequisites may sustain choices even when verified legitimate progress stops; this mechanism is not an observed result of this repository. The [roadmap](AI_WEB_AGENT_TRAP_RESEARCH_ROADMAP.md) proposes matched semantic/topological conditions with a reachable goal and available exit. Entry, retention, safe stop, escape and recovery require separate endpoints. A clicked redirect, self-reflected task failure, tool-call crash or token multiplier cannot establish the whole mechanism.

**Core untested hypotheses:** H1: Task-relevant recurring prerequisites increase bounded non-progress interaction compared with neutral framing on equivalent recurring structures. H2: Task relevance interacts with recurrence, producing an effect beyond topology alone. H3: Appropriate progress-aware escape strategies can reduce non-progress work without substantially damaging legitimate iterative task performance. H1 is the proposed later confirmatory primary; H2 supporting; H3 exploratory. The initial pilot remains exploratory feasibility. See [hypotheses and competing explanations](RESEARCH_GAPS_AND_HYPOTHESES.md).

No broad first-trap claim or completed new attack is supported. Remaining sections preserve the complete 112-record evidence and its quantitative/source qualifications. Standalone comparisons, memory, multimodal manipulation, adaptation and enforcement remain supporting directions. No new experiment has started.

## 1. Scope, evidence and method

This preliminary cross-paper reference covers **112 canonical paper records**. It consolidates the existing synthesis and consults completed structured records and summaries; it is not a new systematic search, full scholarly literature review, novelty claim or independent reproduction. Linked records retain full narratives, typed claim IDs, source identities and physical PDF page locators. Numerical statements retain source-defined protocols. External artifacts were not treated as inspected merely because a paper linked them.

**Evidence convention:** unmarked study findings are author-reported observations under their stated protocols. “Analyst inference,” “interpretation” and “hypothesis” identify reasoning beyond an observed result. A reported demonstration is not independent reproduction; structural/quotation checks do not prove entailment. Survey statistics remain secondary evidence. Missing denominators, implementations and supplements stay unresolved.

**Three separate quantities:** (A) exposure to malicious or untrusted material; (B) the underlying model's susceptibility given that exposure; and (C) consequences enabled by tools, permissions and persistent state. Retrieval, instruction following, proposed actions, executed calls and verified harm need distinct measures. Benign failure and increased consequence severity do not establish increased susceptibility.

**Study identity:**112 canonical records are not112 independent studies. AgentFuzzer's filename maps to an AGENTVIGIL v4 paper related to published AgentVigil; the two Mind the Web records are versions of one study. Version-specific results remain separately cited, and shared findings are not independent replication. Five exact archived PDF duplicates remain outside the112. The [comparison reference](SECURITY_COMPARISON_MATRIX.md) provides one primary profile per canonical identity.

## 2. Agent architectures and external interaction

### Observation, planning and action interfaces

The architectural papers support a recurring observation–decision–action loop. ReAct adds thoughts to context between environment actions; AutoWebGLM combines page representation, task, location and history; BrowserGym exposes an observation/action/reward interface through Chromium and Playwright. These are architectural descriptions, not evidence of adversarial compromise. [ReAct, react_c01, pp.3–4](../records/react_comprehensive_summary.json), [AutoWebGLM, autowebglm_c02, pp.3–4](../records/autowebglm_comprehensive_summary.json), [BrowserGym, BG-C02, p.5](../records/browsergym_comprehensive_summary.json).

Interfaces vary materially. SeeAct first generates screenshot-based intentions and then grounds them into elements. Mind2Web evaluates independent steps on cached pages using ground-truth histories. MMINA uses ordered multihop completion. These settings cannot be treated as interchangeable free-running agents. [SeeAct, seeact_c01/c03, pp.3–4](../records/seeact_comprehensive_summary.json), [Mind2Web, mind2web_c04/c08, pp.7,10](../records/mind2web_comprehensive_summary.json), [MMINA, mmina_c02/c03, p.3](../records/mmina_comprehensive_summary.json).

Two designs illustrate different external-information interfaces. VisualWebArena uses screenshots/accessibility trees/captions and SoM IDs to connect observations to browser actions. Its 16.37% GPT-4V SoM result on 910 tasks is legitimate task success, not ASR; the 88.70% human result uses a separate 230-task sample. Its later GPT-4o SoM result is 19.78%, a distinct model/evaluation addition. WebGPT instead exposes a restricted text browser with search, existing-link navigation and quotation collection, trained with demonstrations and human preferences. Its hypothetical Wikipedia-editing concern is not an observed exploit and is excluded by the implemented action set. **Analyst inference:** these architectures differ in both observation exposure and possible action consequences; browser integration alone does not imply unrestricted write access. [VisualWebArena, vwa_c01–08/10–13, pp.1,3–9,13,15–17](../records/visualwebarena_comprehensive_summary.json), [WebGPT, webgpt_c01/06–11/14, pp.1–11,17–18](../records/webgpt_comprehensive_summary.json).

WebLINX adds multi-turn user dialogue to DOM/screenshot grounding and structured actions; its static demonstrations assess next-action similarity, not completed task outcomes. WebWalker adds an explorer–critic loop, accumulating page information in critic memory and optionally appending that memory to RAG evidence. These define candidate information/action/state boundaries, but neither paper tests attacker control or defenses. [WebLINX record](../records/weblinx_comprehensive_summary.json), weblinx_c01–14; pp.1–9,27–32. [WebWalker record](../records/webwalker_comprehensive_summary.json), ww_c01–15; pp.3–9,13–16.

Go-Browse adds structured exploration and model-judged successful trajectories for training. Its webpage-observation and action-execution boundaries are relevant architectural evidence; the paper tests task solving rather than adversarial susceptibility. Its bounded feasibility attempts and training-data selection should not be read as proof that failed tasks are impossible or as a security evaluation. [Go-Browse](../records/go_browse_comprehensive_summary.json), GB-C02, GB-C12–GB-C13, physical pp.3–6,15 and full-paper negative finding.

Model-selected elements, operations and values become browser events; some interfaces accept raw executable Python, while others map model output onto restricted action primitives. BrowserGym explicitly discusses arbitrary untrusted-code risks. LASER's state-permitted functions constrain the available interface, but its study does not test an authorization guarantee. Thus restricting an action vocabulary should not be equated with validating whether a particular action serves the authorized user's goal. [BrowserGym, BG-C03/C04, pp.7–8](../records/browsergym_comprehensive_summary.json), [LASER, laser_claim_15, p.12](../records/laser_comprehensive_summary.json), [SeeAct, seeact_c14, p.5](../records/seeact_comprehensive_summary.json).

Consequences depend on permission and evaluation restrictions. WebArena describes logged-in state-changing tasks; its credential listing is a setup detail, not evidence of credential theft. ReAct excludes real purchases and edits; LASER stops Amazon trials at the decision to buy; SeeAct uses safety review and prohibits sensitive final actions. These conditions qualify deployment inferences. [WebArena, WA_C13/WA_C15, pp.4–6,18](../records/webarena_comprehensive_summary.json), [ReAct, react_c10/c11, p.10](../records/react_comprehensive_summary.json), [LASER, laser_claim_11, p.5](../records/laser_comprehensive_summary.json), [SeeAct, seeact_c09/c10, pp.9,14](../records/seeact_comprehensive_summary.json).

### Training, extraction and API-call capability

The capability studies provide evidence for shopping, enterprise interaction, extraction and long-horizon research architectures. They expose observations, source selection, code generation and judging as distinct places where errors or untrusted content could enter a system. These are analyst-inferred surfaces, not measured compromises. WebShop’s 500-task evaluation and WorkArena’s 330 evaluations measure task performance; Mind2Web 2’s 99.03% node-judge accuracy comes from 15 selected tasks and excludes total failures. WebDancer/WebSailor training and multi-run comparisons do not isolate the security effect of agentization. [WebShop](../records/webshop_comprehensive_summary.json), webshop_c01–12, pp.4–10; [WorkArena](../records/workarena_comprehensive_summary.json), workarena_c01–17, pp.1–21; [Mind2Web 2](../records/mind2web_2_comprehensive_summary.json), m2w2_c01–15, pp.1–51; [WebDancer](../records/webdancer_comprehensive_summary.json), WD_C01–14, pp.1–24; [WebSailor](../records/websailor_comprehensive_summary.json), websailor_c01–15, pp.1–23.

AutoScraper and WebScraper illustrate extraction-code pipelines rather than evaluated security defenses. Three seed pages, DOM reduction, XPath/code generation and synthesis determine extraction utility. Synthesis can worsen some AutoScraper results; WebScraper’s news/ecommerce results have different task definitions. They do not establish adversarial robustness or a universal advantage of one architecture. [AutoScraper](../records/autoscraper_comprehensive_summary.json), autoscraper_c01–15, pp.5–19; [WebScraper](../records/webscraper_comprehensive_summary.json), webscraper_c01–16, pp.2–6.

Gorilla fine-tunes Llama 7B for API calls using retrieval-aware training and abstract syntax tree (AST) evaluation. The model trained without a retriever achieves 59.13/71.68/83.79% across the catalogs without retrieval; this differs from the oracle-retriever-trained model tested without documentation. Among 100 manually inspected generations,78% have correct APIs and 72% execute successfully as programs. These are capability metrics. API counts, evaluation criteria and tables conflict, and headline gains use different baselines. [Source record, gorilla_c01 through gorilla_c13 and physical page locators](../records/gorilla_comprehensive_summary.json)

### Application testing and bounded crawler analogies

AWE is a context-specialized pentesting agent that attacks ordinary web applications and resets memory between targets. It solves 54/104 XBOW tasks against published MAPTA results of 80/104, with mean solve times 53.1 versus 190.8 seconds; XSS successes are 20/23 versus 13/23. Different backbones and the published baseline prevent architecture attribution. Its reported$0.113 cost per solve conflicts with$7.73/54≈$0.143. These findings concern offensive capability, not AWE susceptibility to injection. [Source record and claim page locators](../records/awe_comprehensive_summary.json)

EvoCrawl is a conventional evolutionary scanner using server-database feedback, manually configured users and fitness weights. It reports 59% mean coverage improvement, known-XSS detection 6/8 versus 2/8 and eight new reports with differing vendor/privilege status. Coverage is not the fraction of all application lines; Drupal totals 97151 versus 61303 and a Dokuwiki ablation difference of−21 conflict with universal improvement wording. Privileged setup and historical version/fix conflicts qualify applicability. [Source record, evocrawl_c01 through evocrawl_c14 and physical page locators](../records/evocrawl_comprehensive_summary.json)

YuraScanner combines LLM-generated workflows with conventional vulnerability detection. Of2361 generated tasks,1818 are valid,667 fully complete and448 partially complete;61.3% combines full and partial, whereas full completion is36.69% of valid or28.25% of all generated tasks. The55.19% form gain augments baseline coverage with Yura-only discoveries; it does not establish Yura-alone superiority. The15 reports represent13 unique issues, with vendor/privilege and potential-XSS qualifications. This measures attacks on web applications, not attacks compromising the agent. [Source record, yura_c01 through yura_c15; physical pp.1–16](../records/yurascanner_comprehensive_summary.json).

**Traditional crawling provides bounded analogies.** Crawl Policy studies conditional URL-availability relevance ceilings, and Web Crawling surveys frontier, deduplication, refresh, spam and trap mechanisms. Neither reports LLM prompt-injection susceptibility. Measuring What the Crawler Sees estimates crawler-visible persistence and coverage under a dynamic-urn/core-shell model. Its cross-projection comparison is not external ground truth; plotted fit triples disagree, and normalization, curvature and asymptotic-inversion concerns remain analyst mathematical qualifications. Policy choice, true population change, missed sampling and identifier dormancy require separate causal tests. Applying these ideas to agent loops or trust selection is an interpretation, not a tested defense. [Crawl Policy](../records/the_impact_of_crawl_policy_on_web_search_effectiveness_comprehensive_summary.json), pp.3–7; [Web Crawling](../records/web_crawling_comprehensive_summary.json), sections and physical-page locators in record; [Measuring](../records/measuring_what_the_crawler_sees_discovery_curves_core_persistence_and_shell_dynamics_in_longitudinal_web_crawls_comprehensive_summary.json), pp.3–13.

## 3. Untrusted inputs and trust boundaries

### Exposure, susceptibility and consequences

**Analyst synthesis:** the reviewed designs support distinct exposure, authority and consequence pathways. Browser screenshots/DOM can carry instructions; memories and installed executable skills extend persistence; tool execution gives an output operational effect. These mechanisms do not imply that every added component raises attack susceptibility under every protocol. Conventional scanner and pentester findings describe offensive capability against applications, while WebChoreArena/Mirage describe benign task difficulty. They cannot serve as comparative agent-compromise rates. [UCM, C01–C12, pp.1–25](../records/untrusted_content_masking_for_web_agents_with_security_guarantees_comprehensive_summary.json), [SkillTrojan, C01–C15, pp.1–14](../records/skilltrojan_comprehensive_summary.json), [Mirage, claim_01–14, pp.1–47](../records/the_long_horizon_task_mirage_diagnosing_where_and_why_agentic_systems_break_comprehensive_summary.json).

Potential inputs include retrieved pages, HTML/DOM-derived descriptions, screenshots, tool results, downloaded document text, email and persistent memory. The indirect-injection study demonstrates that a third party can place commands in inference-time data without directly participating in the victim conversation. Its synthetic memory demonstration stores an injection and reintroduces it when memory is later read. The general architectural pathway is analyst inference; the selected injection behaviors are demonstrations with stated environments. [Indirect Prompt Injection, C01/C02/C08/C09, pp.2–3,7](../records/indirect_prompt_injection_real_world_comprehensive_summary.json).

WebVoyager can parse downloaded PDFs into observations. That expands described input routes, but its paper does not test an attack through that route. A webpage or file becoming model input is not by itself proof that attacker instructions will override the legitimate goal. [WebVoyager, WV-C12/C13, pp.3–13](../records/webvoyager_comprehensive_summary.json).

Canary tokens add evidence about source exposure and attribution rather than compromise. Token appearances after a site becomes unavailable or restricted do not determine cache location, prove a new crawl, establish robots violations or show training memorization. Threshold ambiguities and attribution assumptions remain open. [Canary Tokens](../records/canary_tokens_comprehensive_summary.json), canary_claim_01–15, pp.4–16.

### Retrieval and application integration

When AI Meets the Web supplies controlled evidence that integration practices can undermine role boundaries: attacker-supplied history can become privileged messages, and scraped UGC can enter trusted context. Its 17-plugin inventory and Common Crawl marker deployments differ from functional chatbots. The13/100 exposure result is a selected functional P1 e-commerce sample; live219/300 prompt-leak labels lack ground-truth prompts. Local ten-run cells isolate several role/insertion/prompt configurations but change message layout and provider support; system-role cells have onlyfiveOpenAImodels. Hardening application task constraints does not reliably secure tool arguments. UGCBuster’s30-page detection study and reported40–75% attack reduction lack a full adaptive defense/utility evaluation; absolute-versus-relative reduction is unspecified. [Plugin study](../records/when_ai_meets_web_comprehensive_summary.json), claim_01–14, physical pp.3–14,18–20.

Retrieval Barrier separates entering the retrieval context from downstream harmful action. Optimized placement can succeed while a particular attack fails: the GPT-4o CEM email target has recall1.0 and ASR0.02, whereas tool broadcasting is0.92. Single/multi-agent code outcomes also differ, but payload and framework adaptation prevent isolating agent count as the cause. The missing theorem proof and source count discrepancies remain unresolved. [Retrieval Barrier](../records/retrieval_barrier_comprehensive_summary.json), C01–18, pp.1–20.

## 4. Demonstrated instruction redirection and adversarial control

### Environmental injection and contributor-level access

The indirect-injection paper provides selected examples of unauthorized instruction redirection, propagation through synthetic messages, memory persistence, and injected influence on later search/API retrieval. Real-network, prepared-tool, local-file and synthetic-search settings must remain distinct. It does not provide an overall trial denominator, so reported demonstration success is not a general 100% ASR. [Indirect Prompt Injection, C05/C06/C08–C13, pp.5–9](../records/indirect_prompt_injection_real_world_comprehensive_summary.json).

Formalizing Prompt Injection evaluates naive, escape-character, context-ignoring, fake-completion and combined attacks across instruction/data NLP tasks. Its GPT-4 combined average ASV of 0.75 is a mixed task-score average, not a deployed-agent compromise probability. Role separation in the tested system/user arrangement was insufficient for those tested attacks; it does not show that every possible separation or containment mechanism fails. [Formalizing Prompt Injection, C02–C05/C15, pp.4–6,8–10](../records/formalizing_prompt_injection_comprehensive_summary.json).

WASP shows that contributor-level content can be sufficient for browser-agent hijacking without site-layout control. URL-fragment instructions persist through the current URL, creating an additional state carrier. Its85.7% intermediate versus16.7% final success for one o1 configuration illustrates the difference between diversion and completed harm. SecureWebArena diagnoses initial reasoning, unsafe behavior and final delivery separately; its76.67–100% pop-up PDR range is a final-outcome measure under broader environment control, not comparable to Pop-ups' suppressed-click ASR. Its malicious-user vectors also differ from third-party environmental injection. No cross-paper vulnerability ranking follows. [WASP, wasp_c01–08/14/16, pp.3–9,18](../records/wasp_comprehensive_summary.json), [SecureWebArena, C01_dual_source_threat_model/C02_benchmark_counts/C03_layered_evaluation/C05_popup_success/C08_live_scope, pp.3–8](../records/securewebarena_a_holistic_security_evaluation_benchmark_for_lvlm_based_web_agents_comprehensive_summary.json). Ordinary task failures, inaccurate answers and calibration errors are not substitutes for an attacker objective and corresponding outcome measurement.

### Adaptive attack generation and study versions

AGENTVIGIL v4 adds adaptive black-box search over external-content injections, using helper-model mutations, coverage-aware scores and MCTS-inspired selection. Its AgentDojo o3-mini 71% fuzzing result uses 142 cases; the 65% transfer result uses 173 test cases and selected-prompt union, not a single unconstrained trial. VWA-adv has different budgets and 99/100 fuzz/test cases; selected-prompt GPT-4o ASRs of 60/59% differ from prose's 70% fuzzing claim. These unresolved aggregation and defense-superiority inconsistencies remain explicit. A local shopping review case establishes redirected navigation; wider malware/exfiltration consequences are not thereby demonstrated. The stable AgentFuzzer filename maps to an actual AGENTVIGIL v4 PDF, so it is not counted as a separate named scientific replication. [AGENTVIGIL, C01_indirect_external_content–C14_research_relevance, pp.1–9,12,14](../records/agentfuzzer_generic_black_box_fuzzing_for_indirect_prompt_injection_against_llm_agents_comprehensive_summary.json).

The published AgentVigil record is a version-family member of the earlier AgentFuzzer material, not an independent replication. Its top-five union rates, optimization/test splits and adaptive-defense optimization differ. The published adaptive repeat result must not be pooled with nonadaptive repeat outcomes or treated as a paired causal improvement. Benign utility remains missing. [AgentVigil](../records/agentvigil_generic_black_box_red_teaming_for_indirect_prompt_injection_against_llm_agents_comprehensive_summary.json), av_c01–15, pp.2–14.

The published 17-page paper and undated 13-page manuscript are versions of one study. Their task-aligned attack pipeline validates candidates by browser interaction before offline SFT/DPO. The manuscript uses approximately1500 candidates/~300 SFT examples/12000 preference pairs and reports61%/82% SFT/DPO ASR; the publication uses2000/~400 and64%/85%. Both compare attack generators rather than matched standalone and agent victims. Duplicated payload/model-transfer rates are not independent replication. [Published record](../records/mind_the_web_the_security_of_web_use_agents_5a834c4e_comprehensive_summary.json), C03–C09, physical pp.5–8; [manuscript record](../records/mind_the_web_the_security_of_web_use_agents_comprehensive_summary.json), claim_03–claim_08, physical pp.4–8.

The attack mechanism couples untrusted task-helpful instructions with existing browser authority. Camera/microphone access requires pre-granted permissions; authenticated operations and local-file access depend on deployment state. Automated rates come from Browser-Use, while the other four agents have manual demonstrations without per-agent repeated-test denominators. Operator P9 requires a manually added permission-bypass phrase. The ParaBank transfer is a demo transaction, and Comet evidence establishes directory access/preparation to transmit rather than confirmed external receipt. These are distinct consequences, not a pooled five-agent harmful-action rate. [Published record](../records/mind_the_web_the_security_of_web_use_agents_5a834c4e_comprehensive_summary.json), C01,C07–C10,C18, physical pp.3–8; [manuscript record](../records/mind_the_web_the_security_of_web_use_agents_comprehensive_summary.json), claim_01,claim_07–claim_11, physical pp.3–12.

### Malicious-user and deployed-application outcomes

Commercial-agent demonstrations show stronger action-side evidence in selected controlled cases: form submission and downloading/running a test executable. Their 10/10 results are conditional on specific encounters or setups; PaperQA’s 100/100 concerns one fabricated retrieved document in a synthetic corpus. SearchGeo and FORGE primarily measure malicious endorsement or recommendation under assumed ranked exposure, not completed installation, purchase or compromise. Unsafe LLM Search measures risky recommendations and classifier outcomes, with ambiguous URLs and differing denominators. These endpoint distinctions matter more than a pooled attack-success ranking. [Commercial agents](../records/commercial_llm_agents_are_already_vulnerable_to_simple_yet_dangerous_attacks_comprehensive_summary.json), C01–14, pp.1–10; [SearchGeo](../records/searchgeo_comprehensive_summary.json), searchgeo_c01–15, pp.1–22; [FORGE](../records/forge_comprehensive_summary.json), forge_c01–16, pp.1–26; [Unsafe search](../records/unsafe_llm_search_comprehensive_summary.json), unsafe_search_c01–16, physical pp.1–21.

SafeArena supplies a separate malicious-user misuse condition with permissions already granted. Its 250 harmful/250 paired safe tasks help separate capability from refusal, but functional harmful completion and trajectory judge ratings are distinct: GPT-4o has 22.8% functional harmful TCR versus 34.7% ARIA-4. ARIA-3 attempted-but-failed tasks are not completed harm. All 49 selected, initially refused Claude tasks were manually jailbroken within three attempts; that result is conditional on selection, not benchmark-wide 100% harmful completion. Priming also changes starting state and remaining-task wording, complicating a single- factor interpretation. No matched standalone security comparison is supplied. [SafeArena, SA_C01–10/13, pp.3–8,14–15](../records/safearena_comprehensive_summary.json).

### Evidence contamination and navigation diversion

External evidence can influence both factual conclusions and subsequent navigation. POTEMKIN measures contamination and phantom traversal, while TRAP measures click/redirect diversion. These endpoints do not establish completed credential theft, exfiltration or real financial loss. TRAP’s 948 successes among3780 runs are distinct from639 step-cap terminations. Its 77.5% button share uses successful attacks as its denominator; the paired email experiment provides a narrower interface comparison. Table5/Table15 location aggregates remain unresolved. [Record](../records/its_a_trap_task_redirecting_agent_persuasion_benchmark_for_web_agents_comprehensive_summary.json), trap_claim_01, trap_claim_02, trap_claim_03, trap_claim_04, trap_claim_05, trap_claim_06, trap_claim_07, trap_claim_08, trap_claim_09, trap_claim_10, trap_claim_11, trap_claim_12, trap_claim_13, trap_claim_14, trap_claim_15, trap_claim_16.

POTEMKIN’s contamination and engagement rates use different denominators and cohorts. The defense evidence includes utility reductions and inconsistent Table A5/Figure A2 threshold descriptions. Low attack engagement by an error-prone agent is not evidence of stronger security. Conditional retrieval exposure is an important trust-boundary hypothesis, but this is not an otherwise matched standalone-versus-agent experiment. [Record](../records/how_adversarial_environments_mislead_agentic_ai_comprehensive_summary.json), C01, C02, C03, C04, C05, C06, C07, C08, C09, C10, C11, C12, C13, C14, C15, C16.

## 5. Multimodal, tool-execution and memory attacks

### Visual and accessibility manipulation

AdvAgent demonstrates argument redirection through hidden target-element aria-label content in SeeAct grounding. Its exact adversarial-triplet endpoint retains the operation and selected element while changing the argument. The 97.5%/99.8% domain-average rates use the selected 200-task test split; displayed variation is across domains, not uncertainty from repeated trials. EIA instead injects fields and misleading descriptions to redirect private-value entry. Its 70% specific-PII peak and 16% full-request peak use different opacity, placement and attack strategies. Neither percentage measures an independently audited end-to-end harmful transaction. [AdvAgent, C01–C15, pp.3–8,12–13](../records/advagent_controllable_blackbox_red_teaming_on_web_agents_comprehensive_summary.json), [EIA, eia_claim_01–07/09/12, pp.4–9,24,28](../records/eia_environmental_injection_attack_on_generalist_web_agents_for_privacy_leakage_comprehensive_summary.json).

Pop-ups demonstrates visual/accessibility instructions steering clicks, including when generated thoughts still describe the intended action. Its default attacker has the true user query and exact coordinates/SoM ID; weaker access variants change success. Clicks are suppressed rather than redirected, so malicious-click success is not malware execution. AgentDojo measures malicious tool actions in simulated mutable applications, using 629 user/attacker-goal pairs and state-based checks. ACE provides an architectural contrast: trusted query-only abstract planning precedes independent app matching, static lattice information-flow checks and isolated rule-based execution. It prevents attacker-app invocation in1054 adapted INJECAGENT cases; benchmark utility measures expected-app invocation rather than full answer correctness. In2000 ASB trials, three designated attacker apps are invoked and classified by the authors as legitimate task matches. Those calls must remain visible rather than be converted to zero raw invocation. AppendixB explicitly denies a formal Python/runtime privacy-soundness guarantee and acknowledges escaped-worker or forged-message risks. Utility varies by model, with relational overall81.0/85.7/66.7% for GPT4o/GPT4.1/hybrid; step accuracy is distinct. Caption, aggregation and runtime discrepancies remain flagged. This supports control/data separation as a defense mechanism under stated trust assumptions, not universal immunity or an isolated effect of agentization. [ACE](../records/ace_comprehensive_summary.json), ace_c01–ace_c18, physical pp.1–18.

**Defense success depends on both architecture and assumptions.** CaMeL separates trusted planning from parsing of untrusted data using restricted programs, capabilities and provenance policies. The formal property does not establish a verified interpreter or unconditional protection for every policy/model configuration. Its main o3 utility77.3 versus native84.5 and nonzero entries in the949-attack table remain visible. SecAlign's preference training hardens structured instruction/data boundaries, reducing main Instruct-model attack rates from89 to1% and97 to8% on the specified208-case security test. The base Llama exception14%, task benchmark losses, and marker-output endpoint preclude universal security or utility claims. These are complementary architectural/model interventions, not controlled evidence that agentization alone increases susceptibility. [CaMeL](../records/defeating_prompt_injections_by_design_comprehensive_summary.json), main pp.3–13 and security/utility appendix; [SecAlign](../records/secalign_defending_against_prompt_injection_with_preference_optimization_comprehensive_summary.json), pp.3–8,12–15.

Prismata combines a task-only origin policy, structural-provenance labeling, top-down Biba propagation, masked observations and an identifier-action gate. Its informal confinement argument depends on reliable trusted structure and the permitted action interface; text masking does not establish screenshot safety or correctness of within-permission decisions. Across three main attack templates, meanASR85.5 to0.7% accompanies attacked task success4.5 to23.0%, while benign task success falls29.9 to26.6%. The average is not a universal pooled risk estimate, and link/form interaction endpoints should not be promoted into observed credit-card theft. The21-goal WASP comparison and20-task adaptive experiments have different protocols and denominators. [Prismata](../records/prismata_confining_cross_site_prompt_injection_in_web_agents_comprehensive_summary.json), physical pp.3–10,15.

The residual15 structurally uninformative actionable paths among90,408 labeled untrusted paths are conditional on discarding79 links under assumptions including non-state-changing same-origin GET requests. This is a structural sample fraction, not a probability of compromise. Language-model labels and a single-expert20-observation check do not establish universally correct provenance. Reported component request durations sum to greater computational service demand, but asynchronous execution prevents reading that sum directly as measured end-to-end wall latency. Cached cost$1.105 to$1.623 is across20 tasks, not per task; different cache savings may overlap. These costs and the benign-utility loss remain part of the defense tradeoff. [Prismata](../records/prismata_confining_cross_site_prompt_injection_in_web_agents_comprehensive_summary.json), physical pp.5–10.

Untrusted Content Masking (UCM) masks owner-labeled DOM regions and uses isolated typed-answer queries. On 12 strengthened WASP goals, reported ASR is 0% against 17±8% for the baseline; its main utility experiment instead uses three models and 100 tasks. The guarantee assumes correct labels, four hard templates require user-approved free text, and potential query-output overrides are simulated. These conditions delimit the protection claim. [Source record, C01–C12; pp.1–25](../records/untrusted_content_masking_for_web_agents_with_security_guarantees_comprehensive_summary.json)

### Cross-boundary assumptions and security/utility tradeoffs

**Analyst comparison of enforcement boundaries:** Task Shield checks semantic contribution to user goals at both input and action stages. WARD screens external observations for injection; Spotlighting marks provenance within a shared model input channel. IsolateGPT separates app contexts/processes and mediates cross-app communication and permissions. ceLLMate authorizes semantic HTTP actions outside the agent using trusted sitemaps and policies. AgentDojo's evaluated tool filter restricts capabilities, its detector can abort on suspicious tool input, and its repeated-prompt defense reminds the model of the task. These boundaries target different failure paths: goal relevance, malicious input, source identity, cross-app propagation, or permitted action. A semantic contribution judgment is not equivalent to deterministic authorization; permitted-action misuse and checker deception remain distinct concerns. The papers' ASRs and other endpoints are not pooled or ranked. [Task Shield, claim_02/03/09, pp.4–5,9](../records/the_task_shield_enforcing_task_alignment_to_defend_against_indirect_prompt_injection_in_llm_agents_comprehensive_summary.json), [WARD, ward_c02/04/12, pp.3,6–7,21–22](../records/ward_comprehensive_summary.json), [Spotlighting, spotlighting_c02/14, pp.3–4,10–11](../records/defending_against_indirect_prompt_injection_attacks_with_spotlighting_comprehensive_summary.json), [IsolateGPT, claim_02_isolation_method/claim_05_user_screening/claim_07_scope_limit, pp.5–9](../records/isolategpt_an_execution_isolation_architecture_for_llm_based_agentic_systems_comprehensive_summary.json), [ceLLMate, cellmate_c02–04/08, pp.5–8,12](../records/cellmate_sandboxing_browser_ai_agents_comprehensive_summary.json), [AgentDojo, C08–10, pp.8–9,20](../records/agentdojo_a_dynamic_environment_to_evaluate_prompt_injection_attacks_and_defenses_for_llm_agents_comprehensive_summary.json).

Capability filters, human assistance and omitted sensitive actions in non-security studies are not credited as experimentally validated attack defenses.

The formal injection benchmark illustrates security/utility tradeoffs: one naive LLM detector has zero observed false negatives for the displayed combined attacks but rejects large fractions of clean data in some tasks. Known-answer detection is attack-dependent and its interpretation is scoped to the evaluated prompt. These results cannot support either a universal defense ranking or a universal claim that all defenses are ineffective. [Formalizing Prompt Injection, C09–C12, pp.13–14](../records/formalizing_prompt_injection_comprehensive_summary.json).

The adaptive and context-manipulation studies qualify defense expectations without providing cross-paper rankings. AGENTVIGIL evaluates its selected attacks against specific input-oriented defenses, with nonzero residual ASR and baseline exceptions; security-only results do not establish preserved utility. Context Manipulation finds that SECURE/SANDWICH retrieved-content defenses do not protect directly corrupted plans/history under its tested conditions. **Analyst hypothesis:** semantic relevance checks need evaluation against task-related malicious plan extensions; this paper did not test Task Shield, WARD, IsolateGPT or ceLLMate. Storage-integrity proposals and stronger-model suggestions remain untested here. [AGENTVIGIL, C09_defense_effects/C10_ablation_joint, pp.6–8](../records/agentfuzzer_generic_black_box_fuzzing_for_indirect_prompt_injection_against_llm_agents_comprehensive_summary.json), [Context Manipulation, C02/07–11, pp.4,7–8](../records/context_manipulation_attacks_web_agents_are_susceptible_to_corrupted_memory_comprehensive_summary.json).

AgentDoS reports resource-governance mitigations (download/directory caps and cleanup) without a separate controlled security/utility evaluation. SafeArena's NSS attempts capability normalization using successful paired safe tasks as its denominator, not all harmful requests. Published GPT-4o/Claude NSS values cannot be reconstructed from the main functional totals under the same-pair binary formula assumption; reward/subset aggregation remains unresolved. Reported NSS values are retained without correction. These issues limit metric interpretation; neither refusal alone nor task failure proves absence of all undesirable effects. [AgentDoS, C10/13, pp.13,15](../records/autonomy_comes_with_costs_detecting_denial_of_service_vulnerabilities_caused_by_resource_abusing_in_llm_based_agents_comprehensive_summary.json), [SafeArena, SA_C04–07/11; narrative section14, pp.4–9](../records/safearena_comprehensive_summary.json).

Recommendation and visual-attack defense evidence shows security/utility tradeoffs with incompatible evaluation endpoints. FORGE’s warning and classifier measures include brand-recommendation rejection costs; Unsafe LLM Search’s false positives are a classification measure, not demonstrated browsing-task utility. VPI-Bench’s defensive prompts have mixed outcomes and no complete clean-task utility study. Source/table inconsistencies are preserved in the records and [source review](../CORPUS_COMPLETION_REVIEW.md).

Defense evidence is conditional. UCM's control-flow separation assumes correct labels and restricted query outputs; hard-task recovery can require user-approved free text. Parallel guard mean runtimes do not prove zero added latency for every action. DoomArena demonstrates that aborting can suppress both attacks and useful completion; DUDE and SkillTrojan retain utility losses against relevant controls. These observations motivate matched security/utility and adaptive evaluations; they do not validate a universal combined defense stack. [UCM, C01–C12, pp.1–25](../records/untrusted_content_masking_for_web_agents_with_security_guarantees_comprehensive_summary.json), [WebAgentGuard, C01–C14, pp.1–18](../records/webagentguard_a_reasoning_driven_guard_model_for_detecting_prompt_injection_attacks_in_web_agents_comprehensive_summary.json), [DoomArena, C01–C14, pp.1–25](../records/doomarena_a_framework_for_testing_ai_agents_against_evolving_security_threats_comprehensive_summary.json).

## 8. Evaluation methods, metrics and limitations

### Task completion, evaluator approval and policy compliance

AgentRewardBench shows that observed action completion and evaluator approval can diverge without an adversary. Its 1,302 trajectories span 351 tasks, four agents and five benchmarks. GPT-4o(A)'s success precision69.8% implies30.2% false positives among predicted successes, not all trajectories. Official rules have precision83.8% but recall55.9%; neither comparison establishes attack detection. Agent-specific appendix tables repeat all-agent contents and remain unresolved. [Record](../records/agentrewardbench_evaluating_automatic_evaluations_of_web_agent_trajectories_comprehensive_summary.json), C01–16; pp.4–9,17–23.

ST-WebAgentBench separates completion from zero-policy-violation completion: AWM33.8/20%, WebVoyager12.8/10.3%, WorkArena26/15% under the described pass@3 setup. Aggregate violations include benign hallucination, consent failures, hierarchy errors and environmental injection; they are not a single attack success measure. Risk-ratio denominators and source prose/figures disagree, simulated consent is auto-approved, and policy-load associations do not isolate a causal defense effect. [Record](../records/st_webagentbench_comprehensive_summary.json), C01–14; pp.4–10,26–30,36–43.

[Illusion record](../records/an_illusion_of_progress_assessing_the_current_state_of_web_agents_comprehensive_summary.json), claim_01–16, pp.3–10,19–21,26,30.

Its CUA/BUA comparison changes tasks and resources, so the large rate differences cannot identify one defense’s causal effect. GPT4 ASR-valid rises 23.6→47%, versus ASR-all 23.3→46.8%; Llama70B ASR-valid is 86.9/88.2%. Parsed malicious calls do not demonstrate production delivery. [Source record, C01 through C14 and physical page locators](../records/doomarena_a_framework_for_testing_ai_agents_against_evolving_security_threats_comprehensive_summary.json)

Security endpoints remain distinct: SnapGuard/WebAgentGuard classification metrics do not by themselves prove prevented tool-action harm; DUDE observes deceptive screenshot clicks; InjecAgent parses calls with simulated intermediate retrieval; SkillTrojan uses reconstruction/execution markers and specified side effects. ## 9. Standalone LLM versus web-agent comparisons

### What the existing comparators actually identify

The available records do not establish a controlled general vulnerability ordering between browser agents and standalone LLMs. BrowseComp compares accuracy and calibration across model configurations, with model versions and task-oriented training differences. Bearcubs compares information-seeking accuracy under unequal time allowances and dates. MMINA's standalone-model terminology denotes capability baselines, not a security control. None of these scores can quantify the added effect of browser integration on adversarial compromise. [BrowseComp, browsecomp_c06–c08/c14, pp.6–9](../records/browsecomp_comprehensive_summary.json), [Bearcubs, bearcubs_c06/c07/c15, pp.3–10](../records/bearcubs_comprehensive_summary.json), [MMINA, mmina_c14, pp.5–8](../records/mmina_comprehensive_summary.json).

IsolateGPT compares isolated and nonisolated GPT-4-based agentic systems; ceLLMate compares authorization behavior and policy prediction; Spotlighting tests document-processing LLMs. None supplies a matched browser-agent versus tool-free standalone vulnerability estimate. AgentDojo and WASP also use different capability and compromise endpoints, so lower attacker-goal completion need not mean stronger instruction separation. [IsolateGPT, claim_04_security_metrics/claim_12_research_relevance, pp.1–5,10](../records/isolategpt_an_execution_isolation_architecture_for_llm_based_agentic_systems_comprehensive_summary.json), [ceLLMate, cellmate_c07/08/15, pp.12–13](../records/cellmate_sandboxing_browser_ai_agents_comprehensive_summary.json), [Spotlighting, spotlighting_c04/12/15, pp.1–2,5,11–12](../records/defending_against_indirect_prompt_injection_attacks_with_spotlighting_comprehensive_summary.json), [AgentDojo, C07/C16, pp.1–3,7](../records/agentdojo_a_dynamic_environment_to_evaluate_prompt_injection_attacks_and_defenses_for_llm_agents_comprehensive_summary.json), [WASP, wasp_c08/14, pp.3–9](../records/wasp_comprehensive_summary.json).

A mechanism-based interpretation is supported more narrowly: external data can supply unauthorized instructions, and successful influence can have additional consequences when connected to actions or memory. The size and direction of a matched causal vulnerability difference remain hypotheses requiring measurement. No global novelty claim is made.

The WebGPT comparison distinguishes WebGPT's base-GPT-3 control from a matched security experiment. It reports TruthfulQA answer-quality improvement, but changes fine-tuning, selection and judging (automated GPT-3 versus human WebGPT evaluation). No attacker-controlled injection outcome establishes a standalone-versus-web-agent susceptibility difference. VisualWebArena's text-only and multimodal configurations all remain browser agents. Neither paper empirically answers the supporting security comparison. Preference ratings, truthfulness, benign task success and attack outcomes remain separate quantities. [WebGPT, webgpt_c02–04/11/14, pp.3–11](../records/webgpt_comprehensive_summary.json), [VisualWebArena, vwa_c06/07/12/13, pp.3–9](../records/visualwebarena_comprehensive_summary.json).

Neither PlanGuard nor AgentRewardBench supplies otherwise matched standalone-LLM/web-agent attack configurations. The former compares tool-agent defenses under a scoped injection protocol; the latter compares benign trajectory evaluators. These results support trust-boundary and outcome-measurement questions without isolating the causal effect of web-agent architecture.

WebWalker's closed-book Gemini/o1 accuracies8.08/9.85% do not create a matched standalone security baseline: backbones/configurations differ and the measured endpoint is benign factual QA. Its37.50% GPT-4o WebWalker accuracy and successful-case action count4.67 cannot be interpreted as ASR or complete operational cost. WebLINX's model/modality comparisons likewise measure static next-action scores, with training and representations changing. Neither design empirically answers the supporting comparative security question.

Neither ToolHijacker nor Hidden in Memory compares otherwise matched standalone and web-agent security configurations. The latter's provider setup swap changes prompts, memory interfaces/placement and document rendering together; GPT-5.5IR97/3/1% on a100sample subset demonstrates harness sensitivity, not a tool-free versus agent causal effect. Conditional memory usage and malicious-tool selection must remain distinct from completed harmful actions.

Overthinking Loops controls registry additions in existing agents; ST-WebAgentBench compares three GPT-4o-backed agent implementations. Neither includes an otherwise matched standalone LLM and web-agent security condition. These contribute operational and compliance evidence without answering the comparative causal question.

Toolformer inserts tool responses into generation context and learns calls through continuation-loss filtering. An irrelevant search result can nevertheless reduce perplexity, showing that the selection proxy is not equivalent to semantic usefulness; adversarial exploitability remains untested. The same trained model's tools-enabled/disabled comparison isolates a capability contribution under task-specific restrictions (e.g., LAMA Wikipedia search disabled, QA tool disabled on QA benchmarks). DATESET improves27.3−5.9=21.4 score points, while TEMPLAMA barely uses the calendar. Its one-call cap and lack of chained/interactive tools distinguish it from long-horizon web agents. This is a matched tool-use capability ablation, not a controlled security comparison between otherwise matched standalone LLM and web-agent configurations. [Toolformer record](../records/toolformer_comprehensive_summary.json), toolformer_claim_02–16, pp.3–11,17.

### Status of the supporting comparison

**Controlled comparison status:** the reviewed evidence does not establish a universal otherwise matched standalone-LLM versus web-agent adversarial vulnerability rate. The reviewed corpus supports mechanisms and scoped attack/defense findings, but does not establish a universal causal susceptibility multiplier. Secondary tool-access comparisons require the original protocols before accepting matched configurations. This is a conclusion about the reviewed evidence, not a claim that no such study exists elsewhere. Attack exposure, susceptibility and consequences must be measured separately.

## 10. Conflicting evidence and research-framework limitations

### Primary studies versus secondary literature

Toward Secure LLM Agents offers a secondary synthesis of247papers organized by information flow, delegated authority and persistent state. It supports a mechanism-based architectural distinction but supplies no original matched security comparison. Its ASR129/utility22/latency8/cost7 counts describe metric reporting, not pooled outcomes. Technical labels overlap, benchmark heatmaps measure paper co-occurrence, and proposed defense stacks are explicitly unvalidated engineering interpretations. The survey reinforces questions about long-horizon effects and compositional controls without empirically resolving the supporting comparison. [Record](../records/toward_secure_llm_agents_comprehensive_summary.json), C01–15; pp.4–24,36–40.

AI Agent Traps organizes exposure by perception, reasoning, memory/learning, action, multi-agent dynamics and human oversight. Its Table1 contains18 named mechanism rows plus the human-overseer family description; this is a conceptual inventory, not experimental validation. The cited partial-commandeering, exfiltration, memory and orchestration rates have distinct endpoints and protocols. Systemic and human mechanisms are unevenly supported; compositional fragment traps are explicitly more hypothetical. Proposed mitigation pathways are not evaluated here. [AI Agent Traps](../records/ai_agent_traps_comprehensive_summary.json), C01–C14, physical pp.1–16.

The Autonomy-Induced Security Risks survey organizes autonomy and proposes Reflective Risk-Aware Agent Architecture (R2A2) controls. It supplies no original attack, utility or implementation experiment. Autonomy levels differ from defense levels, and an expected-cost CMDP constraint does not guarantee zero unsafe actions. The proposed combined stack remains untested. [Source record, C01–C15; pp.1–20](../records/a_survey_on_autonomy_induced_security_risks_in_large_model_based_agents_comprehensive_summary.json)

TrustAgent surveys intrinsic/extrinsic components and five trust dimensions. Its 119 references include duplicates or study versions rather than 119 independent experiments. Citation and benchmark-attribution errors remain flagged; secondary metrics retain their distinct conditions, and the architectural hierarchy is not a measured security ordering. The survey supplies no new attack experiment. [Source record, C01–C14; pp.1–11](../records/a_survey_on_trustworthy_llm_agents_threats_and_countermeasures_comprehensive_summary.json)

The Layered Attack Surface Model (LASM) maps 116 papers across seven layers and four temporal classes. It reports 144 assignments, a selected-zone share of 9/144 and seven zero-defense cells. Assignments are not incident risk; the stated multiplicities instead imply 146 assignments, and table/figure defense counts conflict. Its theorem assumes unchanged other-layer observation distributions and establishes no incremental discrimination under those premises, rather than zero absolute alerts. Supporting supplements are absent. [Source record, claim_01_architecture–claim_14_multiplicity; pp.1–58](../records/a_systematic_survey_of_security_threats_and_defenses_in_llm_based_ai_agents_a_layered_attack_surface_framework_comprehensive_summary.json)

The Attack and Defense Landscape systematization reviews 128 papers using seven continuous design dimensions, three adversary classes and six vectors. It separates exposure from consequences but supplies no new common-protocol security experiment. The nine-versus-seven risk count, three-versus-four defense count and review-cutoff/late-citation mismatch remain unresolved. [Source record and claim page locators](../records/sok_attack_and_defense_landscape_of_agentic_ai_systems_comprehensive_summary.json)

### Version-specific evaluator contradictions

The Mind the Web publication's judge reports92% accuracy, but uniform versus stratified sampling and97% positive fulfillment versus94% precision remain inconsistent. A P6 trace scores0.65 despite eventual task completion, so binary ASR can include temporary diversion. The isolated task/action judge and replay/fuzzing defense have qualitative demonstrations without quantified benign utility or adaptive robustness. Its judge calibration and implemented-defense observations are absent from the supplied manuscript and must not be imported into that version. An analyst concern that untrusted action arguments might indirectly influence a judge is an untested hypothesis, not a demonstrated bypass. [Published record](../records/mind_the_web_the_security_of_web_use_agents_5a834c4e_comprehensive_summary.json), C11–C14, physical pp.6,10–11,15–16; [manuscript record](../records/mind_the_web_the_security_of_web_use_agents_comprehensive_summary.json), claim_12,claim_15, physical pp.4–8.

### Interpreting disagreements

Different endpoints explain some apparent conflicts: hidden-attribute argument redirection, suppressed visual clicks, final benchmark state changes and output markers are not comparable compromise rates. Transfer also depends on source control, captioning and tool access. This is a protocol explanation, not a claim of universal agreement. Genuine internal contradictions remain unresolved: Task Shield utility tables, AgentLAB aggregates, SafeArena normalization, LASM coding totals, DUDE budgets, AWE cost arithmetic, Gorilla counts and EvoCrawl coverage totals must not be silently corrected. Their numerical statements and qualifications remain in the thematic discussions and primary matrix profiles.

## 11. Emerging research questions and hypotheses

### Leading trap questions

Does task relevance increase bounded non-progress work on an equivalent recurring structure? Does recurrence strengthen that semantic effect beyond topology? Can a progress-aware escape rule preserve legitimate iterative-task performance? These are H1–H3 in the [current gaps](RESEARCH_GAPS_AND_HYPOTHESES.md), with no results claimed. Verify competitor methods and progress-oracle feasibility before implementation in a separate repository.

### Supporting questions requiring new evidence

These are analyst-proposed questions, not results already demonstrated:

- With the same model, legitimate instructions and attacker budget, how does
  browser/tool access change both instruction susceptibility and harmful effects?
- Which boundaries prevent external data from gaining instruction authority,
  and which prevent a manipulated model from exercising an unauthorized action?
- How do screenshot, HTML, document, tool-output and memory routes differ under
  matched delivery constraints and adaptive attacker effort?
- How do guards affect benign task completion, false rejection, latency and
  utility when the attacker changes strategy after observing the defense?
- Do observed attacks persist across tasks, resets or site changes, and what
  constitutes a demonstrably completed harmful action rather than an action proxy?
- How reliable are attack evaluators and claimed defenses when victim traces,
  execution-side effects, source provenance and response-only judging disagree?

The [comparison matrix](SECURITY_COMPARISON_MATRIX.md) preserves protocol and scope differences rather than pooling incompatible attack percentages.

Further analyst questions concern longitudinal compromise across session transitions, calibrated action-harm measurements, correct provenance labels and useful adaptive defenses. Survey architecture/temporality maps help pose these questions; they do not establish incidence or validate combined defense stacks. [LASM, claim_01_architecture through claim_14_multiplicity, physical pp.1–58](../records/a_systematic_survey_of_security_threats_and_defenses_in_llm_based_ai_agents_a_layered_attack_surface_framework_comprehensive_summary.json), [Autonomy, C01–C15, physical pp.1–20](../records/a_survey_on_autonomy_induced_security_risks_in_large_model_based_agents_comprehensive_summary.json), [TrustAgent, C01–C14, physical pp.1–11](../records/a_survey_on_trustworthy_llm_agents_threats_and_countermeasures_comprehensive_summary.json).

These are hypotheses and requirements for future work, not completed experiments. The evidence supports scoped mechanisms and conditional defenses while leaving the universal matched comparative vulnerability claim unresolved.
