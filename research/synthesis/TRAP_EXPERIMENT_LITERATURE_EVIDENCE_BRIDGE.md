## Competing trap mechanisms

**Established prior evidence.** LoopTrap explicitly includes recursive verification and circular dependency chains (Table 1, physical p.4), task-grounded iterative checking (Figures 9–11, pp.19–21), and poisoned completion judgments (pp.2–3). Its principal setting uses simulated tool returns (p.4). Its published aggregate SAF is 3.57× and TAF 3.93× (Tables 4/6, pp.11/16). Finite amplification does not prove literal nontermination, sustained browser retention, or wasted rather than productive work. The threshold is inconsistently strict/inclusive, and Figure 11 changes the answer between conditions. See EXT-01-E08/E11/E16/E18 and the [LoopTrap record](../records/looptrap_termination_poisoning_attacks_on_llm_agents_comprehensive_summary.json).

LoginTrap makes login a plausible prerequisite and then measures sensitive-information input in a controlled form (pp.4–7). Its task-agnostic attacker uses page context rather than the actual private user assignment. A Whole New World describes fingerprint-conditioned content and reports a small internal Cursor/environment-variable authentication demonstration (pp.4–7), without trial denominators. Honeyval measures engagement of AI hacking agents with HTTP honeypots (pp.4–8), a distinct victim/task/privilege tier. Compliance Trap studies injected retrieved memory and its downstream uptake (pp.3–7), rather than a page-only adversary. Compliance Trap also reports an identifiability × groundability2×2 experiment (Table23, p.23); factorial trap diagnosis itself is prior art. Its observation-footer insertion is not proof of real webpage-only delivery. These mechanisms must remain distinct from one another and from POTEMKIN navigation traps and It's a TRAP! click/redirection persuasion in the existing foundation. See EXT-02/06/08/11-E02/E03/E08/E18.

The preserved foundation supplies direct comparators: [POTEMKIN](../records/how_adversarial_environments_mislead_agentic_ai_comprehensive_summary.json), claims C01–C16, physical pp.2–17, and [It’s a TRAP!](../records/its_a_trap_task_redirecting_agent_persuasion_benchmark_for_web_agents_comprehensive_summary.json), trap_claim_01–trap_claim_16, physical pp.3–19. Their access and endpoints remain those of their own records; the extension does not supply independent replication.

**Design recommendation.** Treat continuation poisoning, prerequisite diversion, impossible-goal decoy engagement, retrieved-memory compliance, navigation topology and observation cloaking as competing explanations. Do not call all of them one ASR. Keep their original endpoints and privilege assumptions.

**Untested hypothesis.** A solvable page-only environment may show an interaction between task-relevant framing and recurrence after matching topology and action affordances. None of the individual ingredients is claimed new. The experiment must add a controlled contrast and independently verified non-progress/recovery beyond the reviewed precedents.

## Task relevance and semantic framing

**Established prior evidence.** LoopTrap's selected cases contrast generic continuation language with entity-specific checking requests (pp.19–21), but do not isolate wording alone from the adaptive optimization system. LoginTrap uses page-specific login rationales while excluding actual task knowledge (pp.4–6). ABxLab supplies controlled observation interventions, no-nudge/first-option/second-option conditions, matched attribute regimes and randomized product order (pp.3–6); these are consumer-choice experiments, not security or sustained-retention outcomes. Machine-Readable Ads links goal-contingent sweepstakes participation to subscription choices using dummy data (pp.6–8). See EXT-01-E06/E18; EXT-05-E06/E17; EXT-06-E02/E03; EXT-07-E04/E16.

**Design recommendation.** Match visible information, factual usefulness, text length, placement, click targets and availability of legitimate alternatives across framing conditions. Separate public page-context knowledge, public task-category knowledge and knowledge of the private task. Randomize presentation order and counterbalance templates. Include a truthful finite prerequisite to test whether a policy merely rejects useful work.

**Untested hypothesis.** Task relevance increases retention beyond the effect of an equally salient neutral prerequisite. Persuasion/choice effects do not themselves establish this outcome.

## Website topology and recurring prerequisites

**Established prior evidence.** LoopTrap P8 explicitly defines circular prerequisites and P7 recursive checking (p.4; templates p.17). Its P2 example can be finite, so a phased sequence is not automatically a cycle. The traditional-crawling and POTEMKIN foundation already supplies topology/decoy-navigation precedent; it must remain in the competitor comparison. See EXT-01-E11/E18 and the existing [roadmap competitor review](AI_WEB_AGENT_TRAP_RESEARCH_ROADMAP.md#3-competing-work-what-actually-exists).

**Design recommendation.** Use a prespecified semantics × recurrence contrast with a topology-matched control. Prove goal and escape reachability in every assigned condition independently of the agent. Record the permitted transition system, session variables and recurrence rule. A repeated URL is insufficient evidence of a repeated task state; a changed URL is insufficient evidence of progress.

**Untested hypothesis.** Recurrence and task relevance interact. A topology-only effect, harmless extra work, efficient stopping, or absent manipulation effect must remain reportable falsifiers.

## Trust boundaries and agent observation

**Established prior evidence.** Ads Table III shows different click behavior for semantic DOM buttons and pixel-only text; Table IV distinguishes offscreen spans, ARIA, alt and data attributes (p.5). The study explicitly warns that framework extraction determines visibility (pp.7–9). ABxLab uses pruned viewport HTML without vision (p.3). WebStep uses a hidden semantic observer (pp.3–5), but its appendix specifies screenshot+DOM input for Claude and pixels for other agents (Table3, p.17); modality is a confound. A Whole New World controls server-side response selection (pp.4–7) and its reported Cursor demonstration has shell/local-secret privileges. Compliance Trap inserts memory into context (pp.3–5). See EXT-03/05/07/11-E03 and EXT-02-E02/E03.

**Design recommendation.** Persist the actual server response, rendered view, DOM/AXTree or screenshot supplied to the agent, truncation rules and observation hashes. Separate page-only, response/tool-runtime, retrieved/internal memory, shell/local-secret and multi-agent conditions. A human-facing screenshot cannot prove what the agent saw; a DOM payload cannot prove a screenshot-only agent encountered its instruction.

**Source limitation.** A Whole New World's broad detection/stealth claims lack a controlled fingerprinting accuracy study and complete execution traces. Ads is an accessibility/choice study; its observations must not be relabeled as demonstrated attacks.

## Verified task progress and state recurrence

**Established prior evidence.** WebStep defines deterministic structured semantic state, typed transitions and GUI coupling (pp.3–4); it distinguishes terminal success, exploration, conditional execution and information coverage (p.5), and localizes bifurcations through shared states (p.8). The hierarchical-planning paper separates planning, execution, postconditions and replanning, but uses LLM judging (pp.2–6). Silent Failures defines utility using required-tool coverage (p.4), which is weaker than verifying the intended end state. See EXT-03-E08/E10/E12/E18; EXT-10-E08/E10; EXT-04-E08/E12.

**Source qualification.** All WebStep terminal success rates use Safe Pass: an informed correct decision point can count without final commit (p.18). Its seven-action Mail oracle accompanies successful four-/three-action traces (Table22, p.35), so a recorded oracle is not proof of global minimality. Coverage is non-discriminative on 226/1800 tasks initially (p.18).

**Design recommendation.** Maintain a trusted evaluator-side goal/subgoal oracle. Report ready-to-act and guardrail-respecting safe stop separately from actual goal completion; do not silently import Safe Pass as executed success. Define new legitimate information, completed subgoals, irreversible effects and semantic recurrence before seeing treatment outcomes. Validate the oracle with known successful, stalled, wrong-target and partial-action traces. Record ordinary repeats separately from verified non-progress. Model self-reports and success-looking tool names cannot be the primary ground truth.

**Untested hypothesis.** A progress-aware measurement can distinguish hostile retention from useful verification and benign execution failure in the proposed fixtures. WebStep is precedent for the method; this application is not yet validated.

## Entry versus sustained retention

**Established prior evidence.** LoginTrap distinguishes LER from sensitive-input ASR and any-success-within-nine PER (pp.6–9). Compliance Trap distinguishes recommendation compliance from generic action divergence and paired damage within the compliant subset (pp.4–7). Honeyval counts HTTP requests, while LoopTrap uses relative steps/tokens (pp.4–8 and pp.4/10–11/16 respectively). These endpoints cannot share a susceptibility leaderboard. See EXT-06-E08/E09; EXT-02-E08/E09; EXT-08-E08/E11; EXT-01-E08/E11.

**Design recommendation.** Report assigned episodes, deliverable exposures, verified encounters, entries, sustained non-progress, escapes, safe stops, and verified completions with their own numerators/denominators. Keep assigned-episode causal contrasts primary. Natural entrant-only retention is a selected conditional description; do not interpret it as an unbiased treatment effect. Predefine the retention threshold and strict/inclusive boundary. Show censoring and operational missingness.

**Untested hypothesis.** Framing affects both entry and post-entry continuation; the study must estimate these separately rather than infer retention from an entry click or a failed task.

## Escape, rollback and recovery

**Established prior evidence.** Compliance Trap's trajectory realignment differs from task-level recovery (pp.3–7). WebRollback's abstract env.rollback selects an earlier trajectory index (p.2), but the implementation restores a recorded URL (p.3). Its benign recovery comparison reports a struggle-ratio of 19% versus 7% (p.5); it does not establish full-state undo or adversarial robustness. WebStep distinguishes wrong branches, delayed commits and premature commits (p.8), and the hierarchical study tests one replanning round (pp.3/7). See EXT-02-E12; EXT-09-E08/E12/E16; EXT-03-E11; EXT-10-E12.

**Design recommendation.** Define leaving, safe stop, URL return, session restoration, goal recovery and independently verified completion separately. Document which state components can be restored and which side effects are irreversible. Evaluate clean finite iterative tasks as well as hostile conditions so a defense cannot win by suppressing all persistence. Charge recovery's additional actions and model calls to its resource account. Keep evaluator-only success/failure information separate from information genuinely available to a deployed defense; oracle-assisted intervention arms must be labeled. Compliance Trap calls its retry result diagnostic, not a deployment recipe (p.9).

**Untested hypothesis.** Progress-aware escape improves the security/utility frontier compared with a generic cap. No reviewed benign rollback result proves this under an adaptive adversary.

## Utility and resource accounting

**Established prior evidence.** Honeyval reports request length, both sides' running costs, latency, token output and functional fidelity, with $10/run caps and different iteration/time caps (pp.5–8). Its Convince prompt lengthens engagement but increases detectability; Careful PI harms functional fidelity (Table 3, p.8). LoopTrap's SAF/TAF and episode budgets do not fully account for all generator, scorer, reflection and target calls (pp.10–12/16). Ads' early stopping supplies a benign counterexample to assuming all agents persist indefinitely (pp.5–8). See EXT-08-E07/E13/E14; EXT-01-E07/E13; EXT-07-E11/E13.

**Design recommendation.** Log browser decisions, HTTP requests, tool calls, model calls, input/output/reasoning tokens where available, active/waiting wall time and dated pricing separately. Count attacker development/optimization and victim execution independently. Define cap hits, timeouts, crashes and unavailable provider counters explicitly. Preserve actual counts rather than reconstructing them from rounded rates.

**Untested hypothesis.** Any escape benefit remains after accounting for useful task completion and the full resource cost of detection/recovery.

**Accounting qualification.** Honeyval Appendix B (physical p.12) estimates API cost from reported input/output/cache tokens but omits higher long-context pricing tiers. Its nominal $10-per-side budget therefore does not prove a strict billed ceiling. Compliance Trap Appendix p.14 supplies 15-step/600-second trajectory caps and 8192-context/2048-generation limits; these explicit caps still do not supply a complete per-condition cost ledger. Source resource limits are precedents, not adopted protocol values.

## Evaluation-harness validity

**Established prior evidence.** Silent Failures reports payload non-delivery, tool-name-only scoring, capability/utility confounding and missing traces (pp.3–6); its argument-level predicates and resolved placement are methodological precedents. Its positive undefended FRR and tool-coverage utility definition still require caution (pp.4–6). The claimed code release lacks a concrete URL in the supplied text, while full suites/per-scenario results are withheld pending archival publication (pp.6–7). The reported re-scoring and null results are not independently reproduced here. See EXT-04-E08/E10/E15/E16.

**Design recommendation.** Before any future experiment, validate clean-task feasibility, reachable hostile/benign surfaces, exact delivery, evaluator predicates and trace persistence. Test the scorer on known positive and negative traces; require it to reject correct-looking but wrong-target outcomes. Distinguish action proposal, executed input, submitted request and verified effect. Use complete per-episode manifests and traces. This document does not implement those tests or authorize execution.

**Source conflicts.** Retain Silent Failures' 39-versus-38 missing-payload wording and displayed 10.3→6.7 with +3.5pp error (pp.3/5). Retain LoopTrap's aggregate/profile/threshold discrepancies, LoginTrap's input/submission ambiguity and absent detailed defense results, Ads' scrolling/subscription-denominator inconsistencies, and the hierarchical study's differing PDDL baseline/plan-alignment values. Version-bound records and the crosswalk identify each source; no pooled correction is invented.

## Prior art and unresolved research gaps

**Established prior evidence.** The original POTEMKIN, It's a TRAP!, resource-chain, classical-trap and recovery literature remains part of the foundation. The extension adds close termination-poisoning, prerequisite-diversion, engagement, memory-consumption, semantic-observer and controlled-framing precedents. A broad first-agent-trap, first-semantic-trap, first-task-relevant-prerequisite, first-recurring-dependency, first-progress-oracle or first-recovery-metric claim is unsupported.

**Design recommendation.** Position the proposed contribution as a prespecified causal evaluation of task relevance × recurrence in solvable page-only tasks, with independently verified non-progress and post-entry recovery, if competitor methods/artifacts do not already contain that exact contrast. Evaluate task families as independent units, retain null/topology-only results, and obtain human review before any separate implementation authorization.

**Untested hypotheses.** H1/H2 concern a causal retention effect/interaction, and H3 concerns a recovery/utility frontier. They are proposals, not outcomes licensed by this literature review. Synthetic-site findings would not prove live-web prevalence.
